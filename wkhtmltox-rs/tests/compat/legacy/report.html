1. Executive Summary

This report presents the findings of the annual IT infrastructure assessment conducted across all six regional data centres and 28 branch offices of the organisation during Q3 and Q4 2024. The assessment evaluated hardware lifecycle status, network architecture resilience, security posture, disaster recovery readiness, and adherence to regulatory obligations under ISO 27001 and the EU Network and Information Security Directive (NIS2), which entered into force in member states on 17 October 2024.

Overall, the organisation's infrastructure maturity level improved from 2.4 to 2.9 on the five-point CMMI scale compared to the prior year. Seven critical findings were identified; three have been remediated since discovery, and four require board-level action plans by 31 March 2025. Capital expenditure of approximately EUR 4.2 million has been recommended to address end-of-life hardware across the EMEA estate.

"The organisation has made measurable progress in patching cadence and endpoint protection coverage; however, the data centre in Warsaw requires urgent attention regarding cooling redundancy and the absence of an active-active failover topology for Tier-1 workloads." — Chief Information Security Officer, response to draft report, 8 December 2024.

2. Scope and Methodology

The assessment covered all systems classified as Tier-1 or Tier-2 under the organisation's Business Impact Analysis (BIA) framework. Tier-3 systems were sampled at a 20% rate to provide a representative baseline without duplicating routine operational monitoring.

2.1 Assessment Approach

A risk-based methodology was employed, combining automated network scanning (Nessus Tenable.io), manual configuration reviews, structured interviews with 43 system owners, and physical walkthroughs of each data centre site. Evidence was collected between 2 September and 28 November 2024.

Findings were rated using a five-by-five likelihood-impact matrix and mapped to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 categories: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

2.2 Exclusions

The following were explicitly out of scope for this cycle: (a) cloud-native workloads hosted exclusively on Azure or AWS, which are covered by a separate Cloud Security Programme review; (b) OT/SCADA systems at manufacturing sites (scheduled for H1 2025); and (c) third-party managed service environments where the organisation holds no administrative access.

3. Key Findings

3.1 Hardware Lifecycle

Thirty-one percent of servers across the estate are currently running on hardware that has exceeded vendor end-of-support dates. The most acute concentration is in the Warsaw and Singapore data centres, where a combined 87 servers are running on Dell PowerEdge 11G hardware with no viable upgrade path under current budget allocations.

3.2 Network Resilience

Core switching fabric at the Frankfurt and Warsaw facilities relies on single-vendor, single-path designs for inter-zone routing. A failure simulation exercise conducted on 14 October 2024 resulted in a 47-minute unplanned outage for six Tier-2 applications, exceeding the 15-minute Recovery Time Objective (RTO) by a factor of three. Redundant routing via BGP peer diversity has been costed and is pending procurement approval.

3.3 Security Posture

Critical and high-severity vulnerabilities outstanding for more than 90 days fell from 214 at the start of the assessment window to 89 by its close, representing a 58% reduction attributable to the new automated patching policy rolled out in Q2. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) coverage reached 94% of all active directory accounts, up from 71% in the prior year. The remaining 6% consists primarily of service accounts pending migration to a Privileged Access Management (PAM) solution.

3.4 Disaster Recovery Readiness

Full DR failover tests were successfully completed for 19 of 24 Tier-1 applications (79%). Three applications failed their RTO targets during live failover: the ERP front-end (Oracle E-Business Suite 12.2), the document management system (OpenText Documentum), and the legacy HR portal. Root cause analysis for each is included in Appendix C. Two applications were not tested because of scheduling conflicts with business-critical processing windows; these are carried forward to Q1 2025.

3.5 Regulatory Compliance

NIS2 readiness was assessed against 35 controls. Full compliance was achieved on 24 controls; 8 controls are in progress with documented remediation plans; 3 controls (incident notification timelines, supply-chain risk management, and board-level cyber training) remain not started and represent priority items for Q1 2025 action.

4. Priority Action Plan

The following seven actions are ranked by risk exposure. Items 1 through 3 are designated as critical.

  1. Warsaw DC cooling redundancy: Procure and install N+1 cooling units and implement temperature monitoring with automated alerting. Target completion: 28 February 2025. Budget estimate: EUR 340,000.
  2. Frankfurt network path diversity: Implement BGP multi-homing with a secondary upstream carrier. Negotiate and activate transit agreement by 31 March 2025. Estimated cost: EUR 85,000 p.a.
  3. End-of-life server refresh (EMEA): Execute hardware refresh programme for 87 end-of-life servers. Issue RFP by 15 January 2025; target deployment complete by 30 June 2025. Budget: EUR 2.8 million.
  4. PAM rollout for service accounts: Complete migration of remaining service accounts to CyberArk Privileged Access Manager by 30 April 2025. Owner: IAM Engineering.
  5. ERP DR RTO remediation: Engage Oracle Support to tune standby database switchover procedure; target RTO reduction from 4.5 hours to 45 minutes. Owner: ERP Platform team.
  6. NIS2 incident notification procedure: Draft, approve, and publish internal procedure aligned to 24/72-hour notification windows. Owner: CISO office. Target: 31 January 2025.
  7. Board cyber awareness training: Commission and deliver board-level cyber training programme; minimum 4-hour session with external facilitator. Target: Q1 2025.

6. Conclusions and Next Steps

The organisation is on a positive trajectory with respect to IT infrastructure maturity, driven by the patching automation programme, expanded MFA rollout, and the completion of the hyperconverged infrastructure migration in New York. However, the concentration of end-of-life hardware in Warsaw and Singapore, combined with the absence of cooling redundancy at Warsaw, constitutes an unacceptable operational risk that must be addressed in the first quarter of 2025.

The Infrastructure Audit team will conduct a progress review in March 2025 to validate the status of the seven priority actions. Findings will be presented to the Risk and Audit Committee at its next scheduled meeting on 22 April 2025. Appendices A through F (not reproduced in this summary report) contain full technical evidence packs, vulnerability scan outputs, and individual site assessment workbooks.
